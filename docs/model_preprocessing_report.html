Part I · Classification data

Constructing the draft-entry cohort

1. Define a stable Combine population

  1. Column names and positions are normalized before the sources are combined.
  2. Measurements are converted to numeric values. Invalid numeric text becomes missing rather than a fabricated zero.
  3. Rows without a usable player-year key are removed.
  4. Duplicate player-year measurements within a source are collapsed: numeric values use their mean; position uses a deterministic mode with a stable tie-break.
  5. Four Combine sources are inner-joined, so every retained player belongs to the consistently observed post-Combine cohort.
  6. Columns that are globally empty after assembly are dropped. Individual missing measurements remain missing.
Why. Requiring the common four-source roster makes year-to-year membership more comparable. Retaining genuine measurement gaps avoids inventing information and avoids “availability” flags that might mostly reveal the collection era.

2. Define the target without model features

drafted = 1 only when the normalized NBA player identifier and draft year occur together in official draft history; otherwise it is 0. Draft pick, round, team, and every post-draft NBA outcome remain audit columns only and are forbidden as predictors.

3. Attach NCAA history without looking at drafted status

StepImplementationReason
Candidate linkageExact compact-name matches in the same season; limited qualifier handling for known UNC/UMD ambiguity; otherwise a unique athlete within the prior six seasons.Keep the linkage deterministic and independent of the outcome.
AmbiguityAmbiguous or unresolved histories remain missing.Do not choose a college identity because the player was drafted.
TransfersTeam rows are summed; primary team and position come from the highest-minute row, then games/team provide deterministic tie-breaks.Represent the player’s full season while retaining one categorical position.

AgeAge is measured on July 1 of the draft year; values outside 16–35 become missing.Use a common basketball-age reference and reject implausible source values.
Part I · Feature construction

Rates, physical traits, and development signals

All feature engineering is target-blind. It uses only measurements already attached to the prospect, or—in transformations that were considered but not selected—other prospects in the same event year.

Safe rate calculations

Per-40 production40 × statistic ÷ minutes
Effective FG%(FGM + 0.5 × 3PM) ÷ FGA
True shootingpoints ÷ [2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA)]
Playmaking ratesAST ÷ TOV; 3PA ÷ FGA; FTA ÷ FGA

Zero denominators, infinities, and undefined rates are changed to missing. No undefined basketball ratio is encoded as zero.

NCAA summaries

Last-season and career values are produced separately. Career totals are aggregated before rates are recomputed. Development features are last-season minus earlier/career values, which captures direction of change rather than only the final level.

Why shrink percentages. A raw shooting percentage based on very few attempts is unstable. The fixed prior adds a modest amount of league-like evidence without learning a statistic from the future folds.
Part II · Fit-time classification

Preprocessing inside the model pipeline

These transformations are fitted anew wherever a model is fitted. Fold-local fitting prevents future validation rows from determining imputation or category levels.

OperationExact behavior in the advanced configWhy
Schema validationRequire configured features, target, year, ID/group columns; require one row per (nba_player_id, draft_year); target must be binary.Fail early on a stale or duplicated dataset.
Leakage guardReject target, draft pick/round/team, IDs/names, absolute year/season fields, match metadata, and every nba_* outcome feature.Only information available before the draft decision may enter.
Numeric cleanupCoerce to numeric; replace ±infinity with NaN.Give undefined values one consistent representation.
Numeric missingnessnumerical_missing_strategy = native: pass values and NaN directly to XGBoost. No median, standardization, or missing indicator.XGBoost learns a default missing branch at each split; preserving the gap avoids invented median measurements.
Categorical missingnessConvert pandas pd.NA to ordinary np.nan, then fill with literal missing.Fix sklearn’s ambiguous-null behavior and retain “not observed” as a category.
Categorical encodingFloat32 one-hot encoding; unknown categories are ignored.A tree receives numeric columns and future unseen position labels do not crash scoring.
Class weightingThe tuned XGBoost parameter scale_pos_weight = 0.3 is retained.The cohort has more positives than negatives; the value was selected in pre-test folds.
Missing does not mean zero. A missing vertical leap, NCAA history, or shooting rate is never converted to a physical measurement of zero. Native tree handling or the explicit categorical sentinel preserves that distinction.

Every candidate receives a newly fitted preprocessor and estimator in every fold. The configured search evaluates 180 reproducibly sampled candidates plus two fixed reference candidates, deduplicated if they coincide.

Selection metric

For year y: normalized AP = (APy − prevalencey) ÷ (1 − prevalencey). The candidate score is the unweighted mean across years.

This converts each year’s no-skill baseline to zero and a perfect ranking to one. Macro-averaging prevents a large draft class or a high-positive-rate year from dominating model selection. The winning pre-test score was 0.560074.

Part III · Calibration and decisions

From ranking score to reported probability and class

Sigmoid probability calibration

  1. Remove six training rows whose player appears in 2018–2019.
  2. Fit a separate XGBoost model through 2017 and score 2018–2019.
  3. Clip raw probabilities away from exactly 0 and 1, then convert them to log-odds.
  4. Fit logistic regression (C=1, lbfgs, seed 42) from raw log-odds to the validation labels.
  5. Freeze this map and apply it to scores from the final estimator.

Fitted map: coefficient 0.448170, intercept 0.308034. Calibration changes probability scale but, because it is monotonic, it does not change AP, ROC-AUC, or within-year ranking.

Annual top-fraction rule

The validation positive rate is 92/146 = 0.6301369863. For every draft year independently, the model:

  1. sorts calibrated scores descending with a stable tie-break;
  2. selects floor(0.6301369863 × year_rows + 0.5) prospects;
  3. labels those prospects as predicted drafted.
Operational scope. This rule is valid only when the input contains the complete Combine-participant batch for each scored year. It is not an independent one-row probability threshold.

Final refit and holdout

The final XGBoost estimator is refitted on train + validation after removing eight earlier rows for players who appear in test. The frozen calibrator and annual rule are then applied to 2020–2022.

Part IV · Regression

Advanced early-career minutes preprocessing

The regression task is conditional: it asks about early NBA playing time only after a player has been drafted. Draft capital is therefore deliberately included. This is a different estimand from a strictly pre-draft college-only model.

Rows and target

  • Input: drafted_players_advanced.parquet, 838 retained rows.
  • Target: raw nba_min_3y, the sum of regular-season minutes across the first three NBA seasons.
  • Rows missing the target or draft year are dropped.
  • The configured target is not log-transformed.
  • Splits: 540 train rows through 2017, 120 validation rows in 2018–2019, and 178 test rows in 2020–2022.

Fit-time pipeline

  1. Validate all configured columns; numeric-coerce target, year, and 48 predictors; replace infinities with missing.
  2. Fit median imputation on the training rows for every numeric predictor.
  3. Fit standardization (zero mean, unit variance) after imputation.
  4. Fit the configured XGBoost regressor. Although trees do not require scaling, the generic regression pipeline applies it consistently.
  5. For the frozen best run, refit on train + validation and report 2020–2022 as the holdout.

Modeling limitations

  • The classifier applies to Combine participants, not every NCAA player.
  • The minutes model applies only to drafted players and includes draft capital; it is not a pre-draft talent-only model.
  • Missing NCAA linkage is retained as missing. If linkage failure is systematically related to prospect type, native missing handling does not remove that selection mechanism.
  • Multiple model families have now been compared on the same 2020–2022 period. Treat holdout values as descriptive and use a future cohort for the next independent confirmation.
  • Predictions are predictive associations, not causal estimates of what a team or player should do.
